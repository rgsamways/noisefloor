Airtime

%

The share of available radio time actually spent transmitting or receiving. It's the field that explains capacity — and the one most techs check last.

Every field explanation on this page describes a reading you'll see on the console, normalized the same way regardless of vendor — this one just happens to be the one that trips people up most.

A radio channel is shared time, not shared bandwidth. Airtime is the percentage of that time your link (or, on a shared sector, all clients on it) is actually occupying the medium — transmitting, receiving, or waiting out interference. It's reported per-radio, but it reflects contention across everyone using the same channel.

Why it matters: signal strength and SNR describe whether a radio can hear well. Airtime describes whether it gets to talk. A link can show excellent signal, clean modulation, and still feel slow to the customer — because the channel itself is saturated. That combination is the single most common "the radio looks fine, so why is it slow?" ticket shape.

Airtime climbs for two different reasons that look identical on a single reading: more clients sharing the sector, or the same clients needing more retries per successful frame because the link is marginal. Distinguishing the two means checking client count and error/retry counts alongside it — airtime alone tells you capacity is tight, not why.

A radio pinned near 100% airtime during peak hours with otherwise healthy signal readings usually means the sector itself is oversubscribed, not that any one link is faulty — a different fix than anything you'd do to a single CPE.

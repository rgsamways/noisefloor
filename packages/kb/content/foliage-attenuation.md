## Technical

Foliage attenuation is signal loss caused by trees or other vegetation intruding on the radio path or its Fresnel zone, even without fully blocking direct line of sight. Leaves — especially wet ones — absorb and scatter signal effectively at common customer frequencies.

The diagnostic signature is a wobbling, wind-correlated signal graph rather than a clean step down, distinguishing it from misalignment. Deciduous trees add a seasonal pattern (worse May through October, recovering after leaf drop), while coniferous trees show gradual, non-seasonal worsening as they grow into the path year over year.

## Layman

Trees between a customer's dish and the tower can hurt the signal even if the customer can technically still see the tower past them — leaves scatter and soak up radio signal, especially when wet or blown around by wind. If a connection gets noticeably worse in summer and better in winter, or has slowly worsened over a couple of years, growing trees are a likely suspect.

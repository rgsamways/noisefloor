## Technical

The Fresnel zone is the football-shaped volume of space around the direct line between two radios that also carries usable signal energy — not just the perfectly straight line most people picture. Objects intruding into this zone, even without blocking the visual line of sight, cause loss and instability by reflecting or diffracting part of the signal out of phase with the direct path.

The common design rule of thumb is to keep at least 60% of the first Fresnel zone clear of obstructions. The zone is widest at the midpoint of a link and narrows toward each end, and it widens at lower frequencies and over longer distances.

## Layman

Picture the beam between two radios not as a thin laser line but as a fat, cigar-shaped bubble. Trees or buildings can be totally outside a customer's direct view of the tower and still poke into that invisible bubble — and that alone can make the connection worse, which is why "I can see the tower fine" doesn't always mean the link is actually clear.
